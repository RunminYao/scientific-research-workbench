# Structural Diagnostics

Use this reference for section organization, paragraph function, repetition, derivation flow, parallel cases, caveats, and figure integration.

## Diagnostic sequence

Assess the passage in this order:

1. Determine the section and subsection purpose.
2. Assign each paragraph one primary role: motivation, definition, setup, derivation, approximation, result, interpretation, caveat, comparison, transition, or summary.
3. Trace the logical and derivational progression.
4. Identify repetition, interrupted derivations, inconsistent parallel structure, and duplicated figure discussion.
5. Select the lightest effective revision operation.

Do not polish sentences before resolving structural problems.

## Redundant thesis repetition

Problem: Several paragraphs restate the same organizing claim, warning, or caveat without adding specificity.

Operations:

- Keep the clearest or earliest statement.
- Replace later repetitions with a short cross-reference or a more specific application.
- Delete repetitions that add no new consequence.

Prefer:

> The local equations determine the near-resonant coupling. The chosen evolution problem then determines whether it appears as temporal growth, spatial attenuation, or bounded conversion.

over several sentences that repeat that interpretation depends on the reduction.

## Catalogue-like exposition

Problem: Consecutive examples use repeated sentence structures but do not expose the organizing logic.

Operations:

- State the classification principle first.
- Group examples by conceptual role.
- Retain only examples needed for the current argument.
- Keep citations attached to the claims they support.

## Mixed paragraph function

Problem: One paragraph combines several jobs, such as definition, caveat, example, and interpretation.

Operations:

- Assign one primary function to each paragraph.
- Split only when both functions are necessary.
- Place examples after general statements.
- Place caveats after the result they qualify.

## Derivation interrupted by explanation

Use this preferred order:

1. Define variables.
2. State the mathematical problem.
3. Perform the reduction or approximation.
4. Present the result.
5. Interpret the result.
6. Add caveats or comparisons.

Move long warnings or representation comparisons until after the mathematical result is established.

## Repeated representation equivalence

Problem: The text repeatedly explains that real and complex variables, harmonic ladders and monodromy matrices, or local envelopes and full eigenvectors describe the same system.

Operations:

- Consolidate the role of each representation once.
- Later mention only the representation needed for the current step.
- Repeat equivalence only when a new consequence follows.

## Inconsistent derivation templates

For parallel channels, regimes, or mechanisms, use a common order:

1. Identify the branches or objects.
2. State the matching condition.
3. Define the detuning or small parameter.
4. Give or derive the effective coupling.
5. Present the reduced equation or normal form.
6. State the invariant.
7. State the physical outcome.
8. Add caveats or comparisons.

If a later case follows by analogy, say which steps are unchanged and quote only the differences.

## Caveat overload

Problem: Correct warnings are repeated often enough to obscure the result.

Operations:

- Keep each distinct validity condition.
- Consolidate repeated caveats.
- Make each warning target one concrete misconception.
- Place secondary warnings in a concise remark after the relevant result.

Never delete a validity condition merely to shorten the prose.

## Figure and caption duplication

Separate responsibilities:

- Main text: explain what the figure demonstrates and why it matters.
- Caption: define plotted objects, labels, colors, parameters, and conventions.

Do not repeat the caption in prose or use the figure paragraph as a second derivation.

## Revision operations

- **Delete** material that repeats an established point without adding detail.
- **Merge** nearby sentences that make the same point.
- **Move** a caveat, example, or comparison that interrupts the argument.
- **Split** a paragraph with two necessary but distinct functions.
- **Compress** correct material that is too explanatory for its role.
- **Convert to a remark** an important but secondary warning.
- **Standardize** parallel cases presented in inconsistent order.
- **Consolidate** repeated notation or representation explanations.

## Structural audit

Verify that:

- every paragraph has a clear primary role;
- each claim advances the argument rather than restating it;
- derivations are not interrupted by long asides;
- parallel cases follow a consistent template;
- all distinct caveats remain present;
- figures are introduced by purpose rather than caption duplication.
