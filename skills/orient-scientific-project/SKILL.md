# Orient Scientific Projects

Recover the shortest evidence-backed account that lets the recipient understand or act on the current research decision without flattening uncertainty.

## Recover the project

1. Read repository guidance and the compact result or progress index when present.
2. Treat an optional `RESEARCH_NOTICES.md` as a discovery aid, not evidence. Use the research plan for priorities and candidate decisions rather than completed-result detail.
3. Search the index by the question, mechanism, observable, task, or result key. Load one matching topic packet and inspect one linked primary source, derivation, calculation, configuration, or verifier. Follow another route only for a dependency exposed by the first.
4. Prefer active scientific sources and executable results over summaries, chronological notes, discussion exports, and generated artifacts. Reconcile stale summaries with their authorities instead of silently merging them.
5. When recency or what changed matters, inspect version-control status and history far enough to distinguish committed evidence from working-tree developments. Do not call work committed, published, or freshly verified without corresponding evidence.

## Reconstruct the mainline

- Identify the central question, the decision currently being made, the few results that changed that decision, the relevant stopped or superseded route, and the smallest active gap.
- Place the requested topic or assigned task inside that chain. Distinguish what it consumes, what it could establish, and which downstream claims remain unavailable even if it succeeds.
- Preserve the repository's evidence labels. Keep sourced or verified results, current inferences, working hypotheses, and unresolved questions distinct.
- Test the local framing: determine whether the work can change the named scientific decision, merely improves an intermediate object, repeats a stopped route, or cannot reach the target observable without an unaddressed bridge.
- Use `$calibrate-scientific-evidence` when a stopped route may have conflated a conditional physical conclusion, a mathematical theorem, and an implementation check, or when its failure scope is unclear.
- Organize failed work by what it rules out and what could reopen it. Do not retell every attempt chronologically.

## Adapt to the recipient

- For a user seeking understanding, read [explaining-to-users.md](references/explaining-to-users.md). Infer the useful depth from the question and explain project-specific prerequisites rather than assuming recent context.
- For Codex or a subagent preparing to work, read [orienting-agents.md](references/orienting-agents.md). Reconstruct the task's role independently enough to expose local-context bias.
- When the task includes preserving a consequential development and the project has opted into a notice location, read [recording-research-notices.md](references/recording-research-notices.md).

Do not require an explicit audience mode. Adjust the emphasis and form to the actual request.

## Preserve momentum

- Do not pause authorized scientific work merely because a result may deserve a notice. Continue through the nearest useful derivation, control, or comparison when it can quickly determine whether the result is a false lead, local diagnostic, route stop, mainline change, or genuinely open question.
- Coalesce that short adjudication chain into one durable account when preservation is warranted.
- Do not start a new major branch, expensive campaign, or broader task only to make the notice feel complete.
- When the request is orientation, review, or delegation setup only, inspect recorded evidence but do not execute project code, calculations, or fresh verification. Report the repository's evidence status and any untested freshness limitation instead.
- Keep orientation read-only unless the task explicitly includes updating an existing project artifact or the repository has already opted into a human-facing notice file.

## Maintain evidence integrity

- Treat orientation and notices as navigation, never as substitutes for sources, derivations, code, configurations, or verification.
- State the strongest justified conclusion and the important non-conclusion. Do not promote local success, an intermediate representation, or one component into a global physical result.
- Respect stopped and superseded routes unless new evidence addresses their recorded reason and reopening condition.
- In multi-agent work, let ordinary subagents return orientation briefs or candidate notices. Only the primary agent or an explicitly designated integrator should merge project-wide notices.

## Report

Lead with the current best-supported conclusion. Make the decision spine, evidence status, decisive limitations, and next unresolved decision easy to recover. Include background, stopped routes, or artifact links only to the extent that they help the recipient understand or act.
